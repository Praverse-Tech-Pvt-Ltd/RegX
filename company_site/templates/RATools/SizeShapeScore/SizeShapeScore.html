IMPORTANCE OF SIZE, SHAPE AND SCORE COMPARISON

        The size, shape, and score of a drug product are critical physical attributes that directly impact patient compliance, dosing accuracy, and overall product acceptability. When submitting to the FDA, ensuring that these characteristics align with the Reference Listed Drug (RLD) is essential for demonstrating equivalence in an Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA).

      A detailed comparison of size, shape, and score helps to confirm that the generic product will be perceived and used similarly to the RLD by healthcare providers and patients. This comparison is also vital in preventing medication errors and ensuring that the generic product meets all FDA requirements for substitutability. By adhering to this template, companies can better align their products with FDA expectations.

Provide data to demonstrate the uniformity of the halved tablets. Data for each tablet half from individual samples should be provided for test and the RLD.

Provide friability data at proposed range of hardness for scored tablets. Please also provide samples and data to show that at the proposed hardness levels the scored tablets can be scored by hand.

Carry out half-tablet dissolution studies for both test and RLD in all test media used for the whole-tablet dissolution studies.

For MR Tablet dosage forms, carry out ½-tablet dissolution studies and report 12-unit data for each of the following media: 0.1 N HCl, pH 4.5 acetate buffer, pH 6.8 phosphate buffer, and all of the alcohol dose-dumping solutions (0%, 5%, 20%, and 40%).

Achieve similar release for whole and half tablets of the same strength. Any recommended dissolution test data must be generated on a minimum of 12 individual split tablet portions. Perform stability studies on split tablets for a period of 3 M at long term condition (25°C/60% RH).

The split tablet portions should meet the same finished-product testing requirements as for a whole-tablet product with equivalent strength.

The assessment should be undertaken on both tablets that are split nonmechanically (by hand) and tablets that are split mechanically (with a tablet splitter). Please refer to FDA guidance for Industry on Scoring.
