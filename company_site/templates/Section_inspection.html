Section 1: Firm Management & Quality Unit Representatives

FDA Terms: 'Firm management' - 'Responsible individuals' - 'Quality control unit' — 21 CFR 211.22 · IOM 2025, §5.5.12

This section covers the responsibilities of firm management and the quality control unit during an FDA inspection. The FDA uses the terms 'firm management,' 'responsible individuals,' and 'quality control unit' (21 CFR 211.22). In this guide, every DO and DON'T is expressed once only — if a concept is about what TO DO, it is in the DO column and not repeated as a DON'T.

check_circle DO
Accept the Notice of Inspection (Form FDA 482) and verify the investigator's credentials before any inspection activity begins [IOM 2025, §5.5.1] [IOM 2025, §5.1.4.1]
CRITICAL

An investigator must present Form FDA 482, the official Notice of Inspection, at the start. This form states who the investigator is, which FDA district they represent, and the authority for the inspection. Record this information with the date and exact time of arrival.

  • ★ Record: Investigator's full name, FDA district office, date and time of arrival — this is your firm's official entry log for the inspection.
  • ★ If a team of investigators arrives, each investigator must have presented their Form FDA 482 — record all names separately per IOM 2025, §5.2.9 (Team Inspections).
  • ★ Confirm the stated scope on Form FDA 482 and ask the investigator to clarify if the scope is not clear — this determines which records and qualified personnel you need to prepare
cancel DON'T
Do not refuse, delay, or limit the investigator's entry or access to records within the inspection scope [FD&C Act §704(a)] [FD&C Act §301(f)] [IOM 2025, §5.5.2.1] [IOM 2025, §5.5.2.3]
CRITICAL

Refusing to allow an inspection is a prohibited act under FD&C Act §301(f). Section 704(a) of the FD&C Act authorizes FDA to inspect all records, files, papers, processes, controls, and facilities related to drug manufacturing. FDA has published a separate guidance specifically about what constitutes refusal: 'Circumstances that Constitute Delaying, Denying, Limiting, or Refusing a Drug Inspection.'

  • ★ IOM §5.5.2.1: Refusal to permit entry is a specific violation — investigators are trained to document and immediately report any refusal to their supervisor
  • ★ IOM §5.5.2.3: Refusal to allow copying of records is treated with the same seriousness as refusal to permit entry
  • ★ If you believe a specific record is legally protected (e.g., attorney-client privileged material or a trade secret under IOM §5.1.5), consult legal counsel immediately and state the specific legal basis at the time of the request — do not operationally refuse without a stated legal reason
check_circle DO
Designate a qualified firm representative to accompany the investigator at all times during the inspection [IOM 2025, §5.5.9.3] [21 CFR 211.22(a)]
MAJOR

The firm may designate a representative to accompany the FDA investigator. IOM 2025, §5.5.9.3 specifically addresses 'Representatives Invited by the Firm to View the Inspection.' This person ensures the firm has a complete record of everything the investigator observes, questions asked, and records requested.

  • ★ This person should be from the quality control unit (21 CFR 211.22) — they have the authority and knowledge to manage document requests and respond to scope questions
  • ★ The firm's representative must not interfere with the inspection — their role is to accompany, facilitate document access, and maintain internal records of the inspection activities
  • ★ If the designated representative must leave, arrange a qualified replacement before stepping away — the investigator should not be left unaccompanied at any point
  • ★ Maintain a written internal record of all areas visited, all questions asked, and all responses given — this record is your starting point for the Form FDA 483 written response
cancel DON'T
Do not allow personnel who are not designated for a specific function to speak on behalf of the firm [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.6] [21 CFR 211.25]
CRITICAL

Everything said to an FDA investigator is documented in the Establishment Inspection Report (EIR) under 'Individual Responsibility and Persons Interviewed' (IOM §5.7.1.4.6). Inaccurate statements or contradictory answers across different personnel are recorded as inconsistencies and may form the basis for observations.

  • ★ Designate specific qualified personnel for each functional area — only they answer questions about their area. This is consistent with the training and qualification requirements in 21 CFR 211.25
  • ★ If an unauthorized person is asked a question by the investigator, the appropriate response is: 'That question relates to [specific function]. I will refer you to the qualified person responsible for that area.'
  • ★ This is not about limiting what the investigator can ask — it is about ensuring answers are accurate and come from the person with direct knowledge of that function
check_circle DO
Confirm the type and scope of the inspection and document it in your internal records [IOM 2025, §5.1.6] [IOM 2025, §5.2.1]
MAJOR

FDA conducts different types of inspections: pre-approval, surveillance, for-cause, and directed inspections (IOM 2025, §5.1.6). The scope determines which records, products, and personnel are relevant.

  • ★ Ask the investigator to confirm the inspection scope at the start — record the answer word for word
  • ★ If the scope expands during the inspection, note the date, time, and exact nature of the expansion in your internal records
  • ★ Based on the confirmed scope, identify the qualified personnel responsible for each area under 21 CFR 211.25 — these are the people authorized to answer questions for their specific functions
cancel DON'T
Do not tell the investigator (or your own team) that this is a 'routine' inspection or predict how it will end [IOM 2025, §5.7.5 (Endorsement and Reporting Criteria)]
MINOR

Inspection classification — No Action Indicated (NAI), Voluntary Action Indicated (VAI), or Official Action Indicated (OAI) is determined by the FDA District Office after reviewing the complete Establishment Inspection Report (EIR). This happens after the inspection ends, not during it.

  • ★ IOM §5.7.5.2 describes the criteria used to classify inspections — this assessment happens at the district level after the EIR is completed
  • ★ Telling your team 'this is just routine' creates a false sense of security and reduces operational vigilance — treat every inspection with the same level of preparedness
check_circle DO
Notify quality unit leadership immediately upon receiving Form FDA 482 [21 CFR 211.22(a)]
MINOR

21 CFR 211.22(a) gives the quality control unit the authority and responsibility for approving or rejecting all records and materials. Quality unit leadership must be available and involved from the moment an inspection begins.

  • ★ Quality unit leadership needs to be involved from the first moment — they control records access and have authority over batch disposition decisions that the investigator may ask about
  • ★ IBP: Many firms also notify legal counsel at this point. This is a reasonable operational step but is not described in FDA regulations as a requirement
check_circle DO
Answer all investigator questions accurately — always verify from records before stating numbers, rates, or frequencies [21 CFR 211.68 (accuracy of records)] [21 CFR 211.192 (accuracy of production records)] [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.14]
CRITICAL

All cGMP regulations require that records and information be accurate. When a spoken statement to the investigator contradicts a written record, the investigator documents this as an inconsistency in the EIR. It is always better to retrieve and verify a record than to answer from memory.

  • ★ If you cannot give a verified answer, say: 'To give you an accurate answer, I need to retrieve the relevant record. May I do so and respond to you shortly?' — investigators accept this; they do not accept corrections to previous wrong answers
  • ★ Never estimate or approximate numbers from memory — OOS rates, batch yields, deviation frequencies, calibration dates must all come from a retrieved, verified written record
  • ★ If the question is outside your direct responsibility, refer it to the qualified person for that function (21 CFR 211.25) rather than attempting an answer
cancel DON'T
Do not make verbal commitments about corrective actions, system changes, or timelines during the inspection [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.17] [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026]
CRITICAL

When a firm makes verbal commitments during an inspection, the investigator documents them in the EIR under 'Voluntary Corrections' (IOM §5.7.1.4.17). If those commitments are not met by the time of the next inspection or Warning Letter, the gap becomes an additional finding. The March 2026 FDA draft guidance states: 'Partially implemented, or promised, corrective actions are not sufficient to preclude regulatory action.'

  • ★ The correct statement: 'We will review this finding through our quality system and provide a formal written response with corrective action plans and timelines within the regulatory timeframe.'
  • ★ This is not evasive — it is accurate. You cannot responsibly commit to a CAPA timeline without first completing a root cause investigation and feasibility assessment
  • ★ Verbal commitments made during the inspection and formal written CAPA commitments in the 483 response carry different weight — but both are tracked by FDA
check_circle DO
Keep a complete written internal record of every question asked and every answer given during the inspection [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.6] [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.14]
MAJOR

The investigator prepares an Establishment Inspection Report (EIR) documenting all persons interviewed and discussions with management (IOM §5.7.1.4.6 and §5.7.1.4.14). Your firm needs its own parallel internal record — without it, you cannot prepare an accurate written response to Form FDA 483.

  • ★ What to record in your internal log: Question asked (exact words) | Time asked | Name and title of the firm person who answered | Answer given (exact words) | Any record or document referenced | Whether a follow-up was committed
  • ★ This internal record is labeled as an internal working document — it is separate from GMP records but is the foundation for your formal Form FDA 483 written response
  • ★ IBP: Some firms assign a second person whose sole job is to maintain this internal record while the designated representative is answering questions — this is a practical operational measure, not an FDA requirement
cancel DON'T
Do not allow multiple personnel to answer the same question at the same time [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.6 (Persons Interviewed)]
CRITICAL

When two or more people answer the same question and their answers differ even slightly, the investigator documents this as a factual inconsistency. This inconsistency becomes part of the EIR and can contribute to observations — regardless of which person gave the correct answer.

  • ★ One person speaks per question — the quality unit representative coordinates who responds to each functional area question
  • ★ If a colleague gives an inaccurate answer, do not correct it on the spot. Instead, say: 'May we verify that information from the record and confirm our answer?' Then retrieve the document and give the verified answer
  • ★ The investigator documents who said what — contradictory statements are not treated as accidental; they are treated as evidence of inconsistent knowledge of procedures
check_circle DO
Answer only the specific question asked — do not add unrequested information [FD&C Act §704(a)] [IOM 2025, §5.1.5]
MAJOR

Section 704(a) of the FD&C Act defines the scope of an inspection. If the investigator asks about one specific batch, your answer covers that batch. Volunteering information about other batches, other products, or past issues that were not asked about can expand the scope of the inspection.

  • ★ Example: If asked 'when was this SOP last revised?', the answer is the revision date. Not the reason for revision, not other SOPs that changed at the same time
  • ★ Example: If asked 'who is responsible for this process?', the answer is the role/name. Not a full history of personnel changes in that role
  • ★ This is not being uncooperative — it is answering accurately and precisely, which is what accurate record-keeping requires.
cancel DON'T
Do not provide records, data, or information beyond the specific scope of what was requested [FD&C Act §704(a)] [IOM 2025, §5.1.5]
MAJOR

The firm's right to protect confidential and trade secret information is recognized in IOM §5.1.5. Voluntarily providing records beyond the requested scope expands what the investigator can examine and may create new lines of inquiry that would not otherwise have existed.

  • ★ If a specific batch record is requested, provide that batch record only — not comparative batches or related SOPs unless specifically asked
  • ★ If you believe an additional document provides context that is necessary for accurate understanding, consult legal counsel before providing it
  • ★ IOM §5.1.5 recognizes that trade secret manufacturing processes and financial data are not subject to disclosure — know where this boundary is for your firm's records
check_circle DO
When you disagree with the investigator's characterization of something, raise the disagreement professionally during the inspection with supporting evidence [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026 (Docket FDA-2025-D-1504)]
MINOR

FDA's March 2026 draft guidance on Form 483 responses explicitly states: 'Scientific or technical disagreements should be raised with investigators during the inspection; if unresolved, they should be addressed in the written response with supporting data and regulatory citations.'

  • ★ If you believe the investigator has misunderstood a process or a record, calmly provide the correct information with the specific document or data that supports your position
  • ★ If the disagreement is not resolved during the inspection, document it in your internal record and address it formally in your written Form FDA 483 response with supporting regulatory citations
check_circle DO
Provide all records within the inspection scope, including their complete audit trails and metadata for electronic records [FD&C Act §704(a)] [21 CFR 211.180(c)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018, Q&A #17]
CRITICAL

FD&C Act §704(a) authorizes inspection of 'all things therein (including records, files, papers, processes, controls, and facilities).' 21 CFR 211.180(c) requires that cGMP records be 'readily available for authorized inspection during the retention period.' For electronic records, FDA's Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018, Q&A #17) confirms: 'All records required under cGMP are subject to FDA inspection. This applies to records generated and maintained on computerized systems.'

  • ★ A printed report without the associated audit trail is an incomplete record under 21 CFR 211.68(b) — provide the electronic record with all metadata (username, timestamps, changes, justifications) as an integral part of the record, not as an optional addition
  • ★ FDA Data Integrity Guidance Q&A #17: 'You must allow authorized inspection, review, and copying of records, which includes copying of electronic data (§§ 211.180(c) and 212.110(a) and (b))'
  • ★ 21 CFR 211.194(a): 'Laboratory records shall include complete data derived from all tests necessary to assure compliance’ all results, not selected results, are part of the complete record
cancel DON'T
Do not alter, annotate, correct, or add to any record after the investigator has requested it [21 CFR 211.68(b)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018, Q&A #15]
CRITICAL

21 CFR 211.68(b) requires that records be maintained 'secure from alteration.' FDA Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018, Q&A #15): 'Regardless of intent or how or from whom the information was received, suspected or known falsification or alteration of records required under parts 210, 211, and 212 must be fully investigated under the cGMP quality system.' Any modification to a record after it has been requested is falsification.

  • ★ This covers everything: adding a missing signature, completing a blank date field, correcting an arithmetic error, adding an explanatory note, changing a number, or making any digital change to an electronic record
  • ★ If a record has a genuine pre-existing error, open a formal deviation under 21 CFR 211.192 — this creates a transparent, auditable record of the issue. The deviation process is how errors are legitimately addressed
  • ★ Investigators compare records provided during inspections against previously submitted data and retained originals — discrepancies introduced through modification are detectable
check_circle DO
Maintain an internal log of every document provided to the investigator [IOM 2025, §5.6.11.3] [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.18 (Exhibits Collected)]
CRITICAL

IOM §5.6.11.3 requires investigators to maintain a 'Listing of Records.' The firm should maintain a parallel internal record. Without this, you cannot know exactly what the investigator has reviewed when preparing the Form FDA 483 written response.

  • ★ Internal record fields: Document title | Reference number and version | Date and time requested | Date and time provided | Whether original or copy | Whether a receipt was issued for originals
  • ★ IOM §5.5.12.5 specifies items requiring a receipt — if the investigator takes an original document, a written receipt must be issued at the time of collection
  • ★ Your internal document log is the foundation for tracking what records informed each observation in the Form FDA 483 — this makes your written response specific and targeted
cancel DON'T
Do not destroy, delete, archive, or transfer any record during the inspection period [21 CFR 211.180] [18 U.S.C. §1519] [FD&C Act §704(a)]
CRITICAL

Destroying records during an active federal inspection may constitute an offense under 18 U.S.C. §1519 (falsification or destruction of records in a federal investigation). 21 CFR 211.180 specifies the minimum retention periods — records must be available for the full period.

  • ★ 21 CFR 211.180(a): Records must be retained 'for at least 1 year after the expiration date of the batch’ - destroying a record before this period independently violates this regulation
  • ★ Issue a written hold to all personnel with records management responsibilities on Day 1 of the inspection: no scheduled purges, no electronic archiving, no server maintenance involving data deletion, and no laboratory instrument data purges for the duration of the inspection
  • ★ Communicate this hold to IT personnel in writing and document that the communication was received
check_circle DO
Have the quality control unit review records for completeness before they are provided to the investigator [21 CFR 211.22(a)] [21 CFR 211.192]
MAJOR

21 CFR 211.22(a) gives the quality control unit the authority and responsibility to review production and control records. This review responsibility exists continuously — not just during inspections. Applying this review before providing a record is consistent with existing cGMP requirements.

  • ★ 21 CFR 211.192: 'All drug product production and control records... shall be reviewed and approved by the quality control unit to determine compliance with all established, approved written procedures before a batch is released or distributed'
  • ★ If the quality unit review identifies a discrepancy in a record that has been requested, escalate to quality unit leadership immediately — the discrepancy must be handled through the formal deviation system, not corrected on the spot
  • ★ Providing a record that has not been reviewed by the quality unit is inconsistent with the quality unit's established cGMP responsibilities
cancel DON'T
Do not provide a partial dataset when a complete dataset is requested [21 CFR 211.194(a)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018, Q&A #13, Q&A #14]
MAJOR

FDA Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018, Q&A #13): 'Transparency is necessary. All data — including obvious errors and failing, passing, and suspect data — must be in the cGMP records that are retained and subject to review and oversight.' 21 CFR 211.194(a) requires 'complete data derived from all tests.'

  • ★ For chromatographic data: FDA Q&A #14 requires that 'each result [be] retained for review' — provide all injection results (including failing standards, incomplete sequences, and retests), not only the passing final results
  • ★ Investigators specifically compare the number of records in an exported report against the instrument's logbook total run count — omissions are directly detectable
  • ★ Omitted records discovered by the investigator are treated as intentional concealment, not as an oversight
check_circle DO
Attend the closing meeting; receive Form FDA 483; record each observation verbatim [IOM 2025, §5.5.12.2] [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026 (Docket FDA-2025-D-1504)] [IOM 2025, §5.5.10.5]
CRITICAL

IOM §5.5.12.2 establishes the closing meeting where the investigator issues the Form FDA 483. This form lists inspectional observations — conditions the investigator believes may constitute cGMP violations. The March 2026 FDA draft guidance confirms: 'A Form 483 lists inspectional observations but does not represent FDA's final findings or conclusions about an establishment's compliance with cGMP requirements.'

  • ★ Record each observation in writing exactly as stated — the exact words matter for your written response
  • ★ Ask the investigator to identify the specific regulatory citation (21 CFR section) for each observation — this is professionally appropriate and the investigator is required to document the regulatory basis
  • ★ After receiving the form, confirm that your handwritten record of observations matches the printed Form FDA 483 before the investigator departs
cancel DON'T
Do not make verbal commitments to specific corrective action timelines at the closing meeting [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.17] [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026]
CRITICAL

IOM §5.7.1.4.17 documents 'Voluntary Corrections' made during inspections in the EIR. The March 2026 FDA draft guidance states: 'Partially implemented, or promised, corrective actions are not sufficient to preclude regulatory action.' Verbal commitments that are not met become additional findings in subsequent inspections or Warning Letters.

  • ★ The correct statement: 'We will review each observation through our quality system and provide a formal written response with CAPA plans and realistic timelines within the regulatory timeframe.'
  • ★ You cannot responsibly commit to a CAPA completion date without a root cause investigation and feasibility assessment — making a date commitment before that work is done creates a risk of a missed commitment, which becomes a second finding
check_circle DO
Submit a written response to the Form FDA 483 within 15 business days of receiving it [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026 (Docket FDA-2025-D-1504)]
MAJOR

FDA March 2026 draft guidance: 'FDA recommends submitting a written response within 15 business days of the issuance of the Form 483. Responses received after this timeframe will be considered untimely and the agency may not consider them when it classifies the inspection and determines whether to pursue compliance action, such as a warning letter and/or import alert.'

  • ★ FDA (March 2026): 'Executive management should review the Form 483 at both facility and corporate levels, form a multidisciplinary team, and commit adequate resources'
  • ★ FDA (March 2026): Where complex observations cannot be fully resolved within 15 business days, provide at minimum a CAPA plan and proposed timeline within the window
  • ★ FDA (March 2026) specifically recommends engaging a qualified cGMP consultant for data integrity observations
  • ★ FDA (March 2026): 'A well-written response will not, by itself, address findings of noncompliance' — it is the actual corrective actions taken, not just the written response, that matter
cancel DON'T
Do not argue observations at the closing meeting without specific supporting evidence — and do not refuse to engage with them [FDA Draft Guidance: Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations, March 2026] [IOM 2025, §5.7.1.4.12]
MAJOR

FDA March 2026 draft guidance: 'Scientific or technical disagreements should be raised with investigators during the inspection.' If a disagreement was not raised during the inspection, the closing meeting is still an appropriate place to raise it — but only with specific evidence. Simply rejecting an observation without evidence is counterproductive and is noted in the EIR.

  • ★ FDA (March 2026): Companies should 'describe the contested facts in the Form 483 response and provide scientific data or other supporting evidence explaining the company's position' — the written response is the formal forum for contesting observations
  • ★ IOM §5.7.1.4.12 specifically documents 'Objectionable Conditions and Management's Response' — how management responds at the closing meeting is part of the EIR narrative
  • ★ A calm, factual response — 'We note this observation and will provide our full position in our written response' — is always professionally appropriate
check_circle DO
When signing Form FDA 483, confirm verbally and in writing that the signature represents receipt only — not agreement [IOM 2025, §5.5.10.5]
MINOR

IOM §5.5.10.5 (Signature Policy) establishes the context for Form FDA 483 signatures. The firm's signature acknowledges receipt of the document. It does not constitute agreement with any observation listed on it.

  • ★ State clearly at the time of signing: 'We are signing to acknowledge receipt of this document. Our position on each observation will be addressed in our formal written response.'
  • ★ Record this statement in your internal closing meeting notes

Section 2: Quality Control Unit — Document Review

FDA Terms: 'Quality control unit' — 21 CFR 211.22 · Records review — 21 CFR 211.192 · ALCOA — FDA Data Integrity Guidance Dec 2018

This section covers the quality control unit's responsibilities for reviewing records and maintaining data integrity during an inspection. The data integrity requirements in this section are grounded in the FDA's Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018) and the specific 21 CFR provisions it cites.

check_circle DO
Escalate any discrepancy found in a record during review — never suppress it or release the record without addressing it [21 CFR 211.22(a)] [21 CFR 211.192]
CRITICAL

21 CFR 211.192 requires that 'any unexplained discrepancy or the failure of a batch or any of its components to meet any of its specifications shall be thoroughly investigated.' This requirement applies to discrepancies found during the quality unit's normal review — not only to production failures. A discrepancy discovered during inspection must be escalated and investigated, not hidden.

  • ★ If a discrepancy is found in a record that has been requested by the investigator, escalate immediately to quality unit leadership — they, in consultation with legal counsel, determine the appropriate next step
  • ★ The only appropriate action is to document the escalation and initiate a formal investigation — the discrepancy is addressed through the CAPA system, not corrected on the spot before providing the record
  • ★ Suppressing a known discrepancy and providing the record without addressing it is a personal cGMP liability for the reviewer and a data integrity violation under FDA's ALCOA framework
cancel DON'T
Do not make any correction, addition, or change to a record after it has been requested by the investigator [21 CFR 211.68(b)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018, Q&A #15] [21 CFR 211.192]
CRITICAL

FDA Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018, Q&A #15): 'Regardless of intent or how or from whom the information was received, suspected or known falsification or alteration of records required under parts 210, 211, and 212 must be fully investigated under the cGMP quality system.' Modifying a record after a request has been received for it is falsification — intent does not matter.

  • ★ Prohibited actions include: adding a missing signature, completing a blank date or data field, correcting a calculation error, adding an explanatory note, changing a result, and making any digital modification to an electronic record
  • ★ The correct process: escalate to quality unit leadership → open a deviation under 21 CFR 211.192 → document the discrepancy transparently → the deviation record accompanies the original record if it is provided
  • ★ Investigators are trained to detect post-modification through audit trail reviews, metadata timestamps, ink analysis on paper records, and comparison against previously submitted data
check_circle DO
Verify that each batch record provided is complete with all required contents per 21 CFR 211.188 [21 CFR 211.188] [21 CFR 211.192]
MAJOR

21 CFR 211.188 specifies the required contents of batch production and control records. A batch record missing required elements is an incomplete record — providing it as-is will likely generate an observation under 21 CFR 211.188 and 211.192.

  • ★ 21 CFR 211.188(b)(11): Batch records must include 'identification of the persons performing and directly supervising or checking each significant step' — all required signatures and dates must be present
  • ★ 21 CFR 211.188(b)(7): Records must include 'the results of examinations made in accordance with §211.192' — in-process testing results must be recorded at each required step
  • ★ 21 CFR 211.192: Any unexplained discrepancy within the batch record must have a documented investigation with a conclusion — blank investigation entries or unresolved gaps in the record are direct observations
cancel DON'T
Do not enter retrospective dates, back-fill signatures, or create records for activities that were not documented at the time they occurred [21 CFR 211.100(b)] [21 CFR 211.160(a)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018 (ALCOA framework)]
CRITICAL

FDA Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018) ALCOA framework — 'C = Contemporaneously recorded.' 21 CFR 211.100(b) and 211.160(a) explicitly require that activities be 'documented at the time of performance.' Retroactive documentation is a direct violation of this requirement and is the most common basis for criminal referrals in FDA pharmaceutical enforcement actions.

  • ★ 21 CFR 211.100(b): 'Written production and process control procedures shall be... documented at the time of performance' — this is not an option; it is the regulatory standard
  • ★ Retroactive entries are detectable: audit trail timestamps, digital metadata, paper ink characteristics (uniformity across entries suggesting a single documentation session), and absence of the minor inconsistencies normal in real-time documentation
  • ★ Any entry not made at the time of performance — regardless of whether the underlying activity actually occurred — is a data integrity violation under the ALCOA framework
check_circle DO
Confirm that every document provided is the currently approved version in the firm's document management system [21 CFR 211.100(a)] [21 CFR 211.186(a)]
MAJOR

21 CFR 211.100(a) requires that written procedures be 'approved by the quality control unit.' If the version in use does not match the currently approved version in the document management system, providing the wrong version generates a direct 21 CFR 211.100(a) observation.

  • ★ Cross-check the version number of each document against the live document management system — not against a printed index or a previously saved list
  • ★ 21 CFR 211.186(a) requires that master production and control records be 'prepared, dated, and signed by a competent and authorized person' — verify that all approval entries are present, legible, and current
  • ★ If a superseded version is found at the point of use: do not destroy it — quarantine it and open a deviation per 21 CFR 211.192
cancel DON'T
Do not destroy, delete, or transfer records during the inspection period — issue a written hold on all record disposal activities [21 CFR 211.180] [18 U.S.C. §1519]
CRITICAL

Destroying records during an active federal inspection may constitute an offense under 18 U.S.C. §1519. 21 CFR 211.180 requires that records be retained and available for the full retention period. Both requirements apply simultaneously during an inspection.

  • ★ Issue a written hold instruction to IT, document control, and records management on the first day of the inspection — no scheduled purges, no electronic archiving, no server maintenance involving data deletion, and no laboratory data purges
  • ★ Document that the hold was communicated and received — this documentation protects the firm in the event of a dispute about whether a record was available during the inspection
check_circle DO
For electronic records, verify that the audit trail is complete and consistent with the process timeline [21 CFR 211.68(b)] [FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Dec 2018, Q&A #7, Q&A #8] [21 CFR Part 11.10(e)]
MAJOR

FDA Data Integrity Guidance (December 2018, Q&A #7): 'Audit trail review is similar to assessing cross-outs on paper when reviewing data. Personnel responsible for record review under cGMP should review the audit trails that capture changes to data associated with the record as they review the rest of the record.'

  • ★ The audit trail for an HPLC run must include: user name, date and time of the run, integration parameters used, and details of any reprocessing including the documented justification for the reprocessing — per FDA Data Integrity Guidance Q&A #1(c)

This section covers requirements for production operators, laboratory analysts, and other personnel performing GMP functions.
