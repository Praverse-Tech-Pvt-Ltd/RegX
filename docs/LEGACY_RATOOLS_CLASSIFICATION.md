# Legacy RATools — Surface Classification

> **Purpose:** a decision record. It classifies every `company_site/RATools`
> surface so a later cleanup can show what was examined and why a removal is
> safe — rather than removing code because REGX has a newer equivalent.
> **Type:** classification only. **No code, model, route, template, database,
> approval field or signature record is changed by this document, and none is
> removed.**
> **Inspected at:** `regx-main` `d323084e211f560289226e268b66b0a50698b825`.
> **Companions:** [COMPANY_ROUTE_INVENTORY.md](COMPANY_ROUTE_INVENTORY.md),
> [COMPANY_RUNTIME_ASSETS.md](COMPANY_RUNTIME_ASSETS.md),
> [LEGACY_BOUNDARY.md](LEGACY_BOUNDARY.md)

## 1. Why classify before removing

`company_site` is a legacy Django service carried into this repository as its
own container. REGX has newer equivalents for much of what it does. That is not
by itself a reason to delete anything: a surface can be superseded in capability
and still be the only place a record exists, the only page a link points at, or
the only thing serving a URL somebody uses.

This document therefore sorts every surface into one of five classes, and says
what evidence put it there. Section 6 records two findings where the obvious
reading was wrong and the evidence corrected it — which is the clearest argument
for doing this before deleting rather than after.

## 2. Method

* Routes were taken from [COMPANY_ROUTE_INVENTORY.md](COMPANY_ROUTE_INVENTORY.md),
  generated by text-only parsing of the company source: **347 routes total,
  RATools 167.** All 167 were parsed and classified; none was sampled.
* Reachability was computed across **both** URLconfs — `RATools/urls.py` **and
  the project-level `RAAI/urls.py`** — plus internal call analysis of
  `views.py`. Section 6.1 explains why using only the app URLconf gives a wrong
  answer.
* Model, approval-field, signature and audit findings were read directly from
  `company_site/RATools/models.py`, `views.py` and the app tree.

## 3. Classification of all 167 RATools routes

| Class | Routes | Content |
|---|---:|---|
| **live-required** | 13 | Public marketing and demo-booking surface |
| **superseded-but-live** | 147 | Checklist listing, CRUD and sample-report surfaces |

### 3.3 dead-unreachable — no live code

**Zero** public view functions are unreachable. Of 190 functions defined in
`RATools/views.py`: **170 are routed** across the two URLconfs, **24 are
internal helpers** with in-module callers, and **2 are referenced only from
commented-out routes**.

Those 2 — `add_appendix_checklist` and `appendix_white_paper` — are themselves
**already commented out** in `views.py` (from line 13254). So the only
dead material in RATools is commented-out text in two files. There is no live
dead function to remove.

### 3.4 migration-dependent

Every data-backed surface in §3.2, and the tables behind them. **Five SQLite
files are present in `company_site/`:**

`db.sqlite3` · `db.sqlite3.bak` · `db_17_1_2.sqlite3` · `db_corrupted.sqlite3` ·
`db_old.sqlite3`

No RATools table may be dropped, and no model removed, before a data
disposition decision exists (§7). Separately, `AIDatabase` has **no migrations
at all**, so schema in that app is not Django-managed — relevant to any future
structural change in `company_site`.

### 3.5 governance-sensitive

See §4. This class overlays the checklist families in §3.2 — the 103 ANDA, VP,
DMF/IIG and Validation CRUD routes — plus the models behind them.

## 4. Governance findings

### 4.1 A seven-level approval flow, stored as strings

**7 models** in `RATools/models.py` carry:

```python
APPROVAL_CHOICES = [('False','False'), ('True','True'), ('Done','Done')]
approved_by_checkers_level_1 .. approved_by_checkers_level_7
    = models.CharField(max_length=5, choices=APPROVAL_CHOICES, default='False')
is_completed_by_doer = models.BooleanField(default=False)
```

Approval is a `CharField` holding the strings `'False'`, `'True'` or `'Done'` —
three states in a field shaped like a boolean. **`views.py` references
`approved_by_checkers_level` 231 times.** Two internal helpers,
`update_checker_status` (29 call sites) and `get_checker_level` (30), drive the
transitions.

### 4.2 Signature images on a regulatory applicant form

One model stores `signature = models.ImageField(upload_to='signatures/')`
alongside `anda_applicant_name`, `proposed_drug_product_name`,
`responsible_person_name`, `designation`, `us_agent_company_name` and
`us_regulatory_agent_company_name`.

These are stored signature images attached to regulatory applicant details.

### 4.3 No audit infrastructure exists in `company_site`

There is **no `AuditLog`, no `log_event`, and no Audit / Log / History model
class anywhere in `company_site`** — not in `RATools`, not in `AIDatabase`, not
in `AdminMaster`. Approval state and signature records therefore change with no
trail, across the 231 touchpoints in §4.1.

For contrast, REGX `AuditEvent` is append-only at two layers: an ORM guard and
two PostgreSQL triggers ([AUDIT_IMMUTABILITY.md](AUDIT_IMMUTABILITY.md)).

> The absence of centralized audit coverage in legacy RATools is a documented
> governance exposure. It is not evidence that historical approvals are invalid,
> and it does not justify retroactively fabricating audit history.

**No backfill.** No synthetic or reconstructed audit event shall be written for
any historical approval change, signature upload or checklist transition. An
audit trail invented after the fact is worse than an acknowledged absence,
because it asserts a record that was never made.

### 4.4 Authority status of the approval and signature records

> **Authority status: unresolved.** The codebase does not establish whether
> legacy RATools approval states or stored signature images remain authoritative
> records for any client or process. Until an accountable business/data owner
> determines their disposition, these records shall be treated as potentially
> authoritative and shall not be deleted, rewritten, or silently replaced by
> REGX workflow records.

**No disposition decision has been made for approval or signature records.**

## 6. Two corrections the evidence forced

Recorded because they are the reason this artifact exists.

### 6.1 "Four dead view functions" — wrong

An earlier pass scanned `RATools/urls.py` alone and reported `home`,
`about_alpesh`, `privacy_policy` and `terms_of_use` as unroutable dead code.

They are routed from the **project-level** `RAAI/urls.py`, which imports them
directly from `RATools.views`:

```python
from RATools.views import about_alpesh, home, terms_of_use, privacy_policy
path('', home),
path('about-alpesh/', about_alpesh),
path("termsofuse/", terms_of_use),
path("privacy-policy/", privacy_policy),
```

**`home` serves the site root.** Removing these four would have taken down `/`,
`/termsofuse/` and `/privacy-policy/` — the last two being linked from
`AdminMaster/login.html`, `HomePage.html` and `contactus.html`. All four are
**live-required**.

An app-level URLconf scan is not a reachability test for a Django project whose
root URLconf imports views directly.

### 6.2 "A third of RATools is unauthenticated" — misleading

56 of 167 routes carry no `@login_required`. That is a statement about routes,
not about data: the views gate records internally (§3.2). The surface is a
public preview, not an open data endpoint.
